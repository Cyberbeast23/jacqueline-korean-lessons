✍️ Writing Day 6: Problem-Solution (When the Weather Ruins the Plan)

Jacqueline, it's problem-solution week again (last time you rescued a traveller who was lost in Seoul). This week's problem comes from the sky: a cold, rainy, windy forecast that threatens a big autumn plan, just like Mina's leaf-viewing hike in the reading. Name the problem, explain why it matters, propose realistic solutions with reasons, and finish strong.

Part 1: The Lesson

Problem → Why it matters → Solutions → Conclusion

A strong problem-solution paragraph does more than complain about the weather. It shows the reader the problem clearly, then offers realistic fixes and explains why they would work. Level up this week: answer an objection. After a solution, add one sentence that admits a weakness and shows why the solution still works ("Some people may say…; however,…").

1. Problem: state it in one clear topic sentence
2. Cause / effect: why it happens and who it hurts
3. Solution 1: a specific fix + reason
4. Solution 2: another fix + example, and answer an objection
5. Conclusion: why the solutions are worth trying

Useful linking words: because, since, as a result, one solution is, another option, for example, some people may argue, however, even so, therefore.

Model Problem-Solution Paragraph

Problem (topic sentence)Cause / effectSolutions + reasonsConclusion

A cold, rainy forecast can threaten a school's autumn field trip to see the 단풍 (autumn leaves). Late-autumn weather changes quickly, and students who are wet and chilled may stop enjoying the trip or even get sick; as a result, teachers are often tempted to cancel. One solution is to send families a packing list a week ahead, including warm layers, a rain jacket, and a folding umbrella, because students who dress for the weather stay comfortable much longer. Another option is to check the hour-by-hour forecast and start the trip after the rain stops, choosing a short, sheltered trail. Some people may argue that a later start leaves less time to explore; however, two dry, happy hours are better than four soggy ones. With smart planning, a gloomy forecast does not have to cancel the adventure; it just changes the way the class gets there.

Notice: Each solution is specific and comes with a reason (because…), and the writer answers an objection with however. That is what makes it convincing.
