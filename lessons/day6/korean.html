Welcome back, Jacqueline! Grab a jacket — it's 가을 (autumn) in Korea, and this week we're talking about the weather and the seasons. You'll learn 10 new weather words with Hangul, Revised Romanization, English meaning, and listen-and-say practice for every word. By the end you can say whether it's hot or cold, talk about rain, snow, and wind, ask if someone has an umbrella, and admire the 단풍 (autumn leaves). Example sentences reuse earlier words (너무, 있어요, 맛있어요) so you keep them fresh.

How to read Revised Romanization:
  • Letters approximate the sound in English. Korean does not use tone numbers like Cantonese.
  • Example: chuwoyo — roughly choo-wuh-yo. The vowel ㅝ (wo) is ㅜ (u) + ㅓ (eo) blended into one quick "wuh". 더워요 (deowoyo, it's hot) uses the same ending, so cold and hot rhyme: 추워요 / 더워요.
  • Tense consonants again: ㅆ (ss) in 날씨 (nalssi) is the tight, sharp "s" you met in 싸요 last week. Say nal, then a crisp ssi.
  • Sound change: when a word ends in a consonant and the next part starts with a vowel, the consonant slides over. 가을 + 에 = 가을에, said gaeure; 바람 + 이 = 바람이, said barami; 단풍 + 이 = danpungi.
  • Listening tip: Tap Hear model, say the word out loud three times, then Record and compare.

Culture tip: Korea is proud of its four clear seasons (사계절, sagyejeol): a hot, rainy summer with the 장마 (jangma) monsoon, and a cold, dry winter with icy winds from Siberia. Autumn is many people's favourite: from mid-October to early November, crowds go 단풍 구경 (danpung gugyeong), leaf-viewing, and TV weather reports even forecast when the leaves will peak, starting at Seoraksan in the north and moving south. Late autumn is also 김장 (gimjang) season, when families gather to make huge batches of kimchi for the winter. A common small-talk opener? 오늘 날씨 좋네요! (Nice weather today!)
