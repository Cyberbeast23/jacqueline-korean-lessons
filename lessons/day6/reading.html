Chasing the Red Leaves

For weeks, Mina had been waiting for one thing: danpung, the autumn leaves. Every evening on the TV news in Seoul, a weather reporter stood in front of a map of Korea covered in little red and orange dots. The dots showed where the leaves had turned, and each week they crept a little farther south. "When the dots reach Seoraksan's peak," Grandma Kim said, "the whole mountain catches fire." Mina knew she meant colour, not flames, but she still pictured it glowing.

Uncle Joon promised to take them hiking on Saturday. On Friday night, Mina checked the forecast on Grandma's phone and her heart sank: 비 (rain) in the morning, a strong 바람 (wind) in the afternoon, and a low of just 2 °C. "We can't go," she groaned. "The leaves will all blow away, and we'll freeze."

Grandma Kim did not look worried. She pulled a basket from the closet and began laying things out on the floor: thin wool shirts, fleece jackets, rain shells, gloves, and three folding umbrellas. "Koreans have four seasons," she said, "and every one of them has taught us something. Autumn teaches us to dress in layers. If you are warm, you take one off. If you are cold, you put one back on." She also packed a thermos of hot barley tea and a bag of roasted chestnuts.

Uncle Joon had his own idea. He looked at the hour-by-hour forecast and noticed that the rain would stop by ten o'clock. Instead of starting at dawn, they would leave later and choose a shorter, lower trail through a valley, where the cliffs would block much of the wind. "The peak can wait for another year," he said. "The valley leaves are just as beautiful, and they're closer to the ground where we can see them."

On Saturday morning, the bus to the mountain was damp and full of hikers in bright jackets. By the time they reached the trailhead, the clouds were breaking apart. The air was brisk, cold enough to make Mina's cheeks sting, but she was cozy in her three layers. Then they turned a corner and she stopped walking. Maple trees lined both sides of a rushing stream, and their leaves were so vivid that they seemed to glow: scarlet, gold, and a deep orange like persimmons. Fallen leaves floated in the water like tiny boats.

"단풍이 정말 예뻐요," Mina whispered. The autumn leaves are really pretty. Uncle Joon grinned and handed her a cup of steaming tea.

On the way home, Mina thought about how close she had come to giving up. The forecast had not been wrong; the weather really was cold and wet. But by planning carefully, choosing a better route, and packing the right clothes, her family had turned a bad forecast into one of the best days of her trip. She decided that next time the weather looked gloomy, she would ask a different question: not "Can we go?" but "How can we go?"
