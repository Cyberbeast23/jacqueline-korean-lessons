🧮 Math Day 6: Autumn Weather Math

안녕하세요, Jacqueline! It's 가을 (autumn) in Korea: cool mornings, windy afternoons, and mountains turning red and gold with 단풍 (autumn leaves). This week you'll be a weather forecaster and use integers (temperatures below zero), Celsius ↔ Fahrenheit formulas, the mean, percents, rates, and unit conversions to understand the weather.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver:
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations, and in what order?
  3. Solve: One step at a time; write each step down.
  4. Check: Is the answer reasonable? Did you answer the question asked?

Worked Example 1: Temperature change with negatives

At dawn in Seoul it is −3 °C. By the afternoon it is 8 °C. How many degrees did it warm up?

Change = final − start = 8 − (−3) = 8 + 3 = 11 °C.

Picture a thermometer: 3 degrees up to 0, then 8 more = 11.

Worked Example 2: Celsius → Fahrenheit

The formula is F = (9/5) × C + 32. Convert 20 °C.

9/5 × 20 = 36. Then 36 + 32 = 68 °F.

Worked Example 3: Fahrenheit → Celsius

Work backwards: C = (F − 32) × 5/9. Convert 50 °F.

50 − 32 = 18. Then 18 × 5/9 = 10 °C. (Undo the +32 first, then undo the × 9/5.)

Worked Example 4: Mean temperature

Daily highs this week were 12, 9, 7, 10 °C. What is the mean?

Sum = 12 + 9 + 7 + 10 = 38. Mean = 38 ÷ 4 = 9.5 °C.

Worked Example 5: Converting a rate (km/h → m/s)

The wind (바람) blows at 18 km/h. How many metres per second is that?

18 km = 18,000 m and 1 hour = 3,600 s. 18,000 ÷ 3,600 = 5 m/s. (Shortcut: divide km/h by 3.6.)
