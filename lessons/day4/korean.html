🇰🇷 Korean Day 4: Travel & Getting Around Seoul

Welcome back, Jacqueline! Pack your bag — this week we're travelling around Seoul by subway, bus, and train. You'll learn 10 new travel words with Hangul, Revised Romanization, English meaning, and listen-and-say practice for every word. By the end you can ask 지하철역이 어디예요? (Where is the subway station?), buy a ticket, and follow left/right directions. Example sentences reuse earlier words (주세요, 감사합니다, 가족, 친구, 학교, 식당) so you keep them fresh.

How to read Revised Romanization:
  • Letters approximate the sound in English. Korean does not use tone numbers like Cantonese.
  • Example: oenjjok — roughly wen-jjok. oe sounds like "we" in wet, and eu (in beoseu, oreunjjok) is a short "uh" said with flat, unrounded lips.
  • Tense consonants: ㅉ (jj) in 왼쪽 and 오른쪽 is a "tight" j — tense your throat, no puff of air, like a sharper, tighter "j" in jump with no breath behind it. Compare ㅈ (j) in 지하철: softer and more relaxed.
  • Sound change: 지하철역 is written jihacheol + yeok but said jihacheollyeok — an extra ㄹ (l) sound slips in before 역. And when 역 is followed by 이, the ㄱ slides over: 역이 = yeogi.
  • Listening tip: Tap Hear model, say the word out loud three times, then Record and compare.

Culture tip: In Seoul, almost everyone taps a rechargeable T-money card (티머니) on the subway and bus — you can buy and top it up at convenience stores and station machines. Subway lines are numbered and colour-coded (Line 2 is green and loops around the city), and every station has a number, so signs are easy to follow even before you can read every word. Announcements are in Korean and English. Be polite: leave the priority seats (often pink or marked at the ends of each car) for seniors, pregnant passengers, and people with disabilities, and keep phone calls quiet.
