✍️ Writing Day 4: Problem-Solution (Lost in Seoul)

Jacqueline, you're back to problem-solution writing (you first tried it on Day 2 with the Chuseok Puzzle). This time the problem is one every traveller fears: getting on the wrong subway line in a city where you can't read every sign yet. Name the problem, explain why it matters, propose realistic solutions with reasons, and finish strong.

Part 1: The Lesson

Problem → Why it matters → Solutions → Conclusion

A strong problem-solution paragraph does more than complain. It shows the reader the problem clearly, then offers realistic fixes and explains why they would work. Level up this week: make each solution specific (not "be careful," but "check the last-station name on the platform sign").

1. Problem: state it in one clear topic sentence
2. Cause / effect: why it happens and who it hurts
3. Solution 1: a specific fix + reason
4. Solution 2: another fix + example or evidence
5. Conclusion: why the solutions are worth trying

Useful linking words: because, since, as a result, one solution is, another option, for example, instead, this would, therefore, however.

Model Problem-Solution Paragraph

Problem (topic sentence)Cause / effectSolutions + reasonsConclusion

Getting on the wrong subway line in Seoul can turn an exciting day of sightseeing into a stressful one. Many stations serve two or three lines, and trains on the same line run in opposite directions, so a hurried visitor can easily board the wrong one; as a result, she may miss a meeting, waste time, or feel too embarrassed to ask for help. One solution is to read the last-station name on the platform sign before boarding, because that name shows which direction the train is heading. Another option is to learn one simple question, such as 지하철역이 어디예요? (Where is the subway station?), since station staff and many residents are happy to point a visitor the right way. With a little preparation and the courage to ask, a wrong turn becomes a short detour instead of a disaster.

Notice: Each solution is specific and comes with a reason (because…, since…). That is what makes it convincing.
