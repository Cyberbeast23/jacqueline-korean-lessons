This time you'll use the counting principle, elapsed years, percent increase, unit rates, student-teacher ratios, mean/median/range, rectangle area, equations, BEDMAS, fractions of a group, money, and elapsed time to explore King Sejong's alphabet and a day at a Korean school.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver:
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations, and in what order?
  3. Solve: One step at a time; write each step down.
  4. Check: Is the answer reasonable? Did you answer the question asked?

Worked Example 1: The counting principle (building syllables)

Every Hangeul syllable block starts with a consonant and has a vowel. Suppose you know 5 consonants and 4 vowels. How many different consonant + vowel blocks can you make?

Each of the 5 consonants can pair with each of the 4 vowels.

5 × 4 = 20 blocks. (Multiply the choices — don't add them!)

Worked Example 2: Elapsed years

King Sejong's team finished Hangeul in 1443. How many years had passed by 2026?

2026 − 1443 = 583 years.

Worked Example 3: Percent increase

A Saturday Korean school grew from 50 students to 65. What is the percent increase?

Increase = 65 − 50 = 15. Percent increase = 15 ÷ original 50 = 0.30 = 30%.

Same rule as percent decrease: always divide by the starting amount.

Worked Example 4: Ratio to a total

A school has a student-to-teacher ratio of 18 : 1. It has 540 students. How many teachers?

540 ÷ 18 = 30 teachers.

Worked Example 5: Elapsed time across noon

Class starts at 8:50 a.m. and ends at 2:30 p.m. How long is the school day?

8:50 → 1:50 p.m. is 5 hours. 1:50 → 2:30 is 40 minutes.

Total = 5 h 40 min = 5 × 60 + 40 = 340 minutes.
