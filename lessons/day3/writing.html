✍️ Writing Day 3: Descriptive Writing (A Hangeul Day Classroom)

Jacqueline, this week you're back to descriptive writing, but with a new challenge: describing a place and a moment so clearly that the reader feels they are standing in a Korean classroom on Hangeul Day. Aim for a topic sentence, vivid sensory details, a comparison (simile), and a strong ending.

Part 1: The Lesson

Describe = put the reader inside the moment

Last time you used the five senses. This time, add a simile (a comparison using like or as) and strong verbs (glide, scratch, drift) instead of plain ones (go, make, do).

See: ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ chalked across the board, black ink drying
Hear: students chanting 가, 나, 다, brushes whispering
Smell: fresh paper, pencil shavings, ink
Touch: soft brush tip, rough hanji paper
Taste: a sweet honey cookie the teacher shares at break

Useful linking and place words: at the front, along the window, beside me, meanwhile, as, while, like, as … as.

Model Descriptive Paragraph

Topic sentenceSensory detailsSimile + strong verbsConclusion

On Hangeul Day, our Saturday Korean classroom hums like a small, busy workshop. At the front, Seonsaengnim has chalked ㄱ, ㄴ, and ㅁ across the board, and the air smells of fresh paper and pencil shavings. Beside me, my friend dips a brush into black ink and glides it across rough hanji paper, each stroke as careful as a tightrope walker's step. Meanwhile, twelve voices chant "가, 나, 다, 라" until the syllables bounce off the windows like ping-pong balls. By the end of class, every desk holds a name written in Hangeul, still shining and wet, like a tiny gift from King Sejong.

Notice: The similes (like a workshop, as careful as a tightrope walker's step) help the reader picture something new by comparing it to something familiar.

Part 2: Spot Strong Description

For each sentence, choose whether it is mainly descriptive (vivid senses/details), plain/telling (vague), or off-topic.
