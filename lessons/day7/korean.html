🇰🇷 Korean Day 7: Health & the Body

Welcome back, Jacqueline! This week is all about 건강 (geongang, health) and the body. You'll learn 10 new words with Hangul, Revised Romanization, English meaning, and listen-and-say practice for every word. By the end you can name body parts, say what hurts, tell someone you're going to the clinic or taking medicine, and reassure a friend with 괜찮아요 (I'm okay). Example sentences reuse earlier words (어디, 가요, 선생님) so you keep them fresh.

How to read Revised Romanization:
  • Letters approximate the sound in English. Korean does not use tone numbers like Cantonese.
  • Example: gwaenchanayo — roughly gwen-chan-a-yo. The vowel ㅙ (wae) is a quick "gweh". The ㅎ in 괜찮아요 goes silent, so you never hear an "h": say it smoothly in four beats.
  • The vowel ㅢ (ui) in 의사 (uisa) glides from "eu" (lips flat) to "ee". Say eu-ee quickly, then sa.
  • Sound change: a final consonant slides into a following vowel. 손 + 을 = 손을, said soneul; 약 + 을 = 약을, said yageul (the k softens to g); 병원 + 에 = byeongwone.
  • Listening tip: Tap Hear model, say the word out loud three times, then Record and compare.

Culture tip: In Korea, many people visit a local 의원 (uiwon) clinic for everyday aches, and some also visit a 한의원 (hanuiwon), a traditional Korean medicine clinic that uses acupuncture and herbal teas. After a long week, families love the 찜질방 (jjimjilbang), a public bathhouse with hot rooms and heated floors where people relax in matching cotton outfits, eat boiled eggs, and drink sweet rice punch (식혜, sikhye). And when someone looks tired or sick, friends say 몸조심하세요 (momjosimhaseyo): "take care of your body!"
