🧮 Math Day 7: Clinic Math

안녕하세요, Jacqueline! This week's theme is 건강 (health) and the body. Doctors, nurses, and chiropractors use math all day long: counting heartbeats, working out medicine doses, tracking sleep and steps. You'll use rates, ratios, unit conversions, the mean, and percents to think like a health professional.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver:
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations, and in what order?
  3. Solve: One step at a time; write each step down.
  4. Check: Is the answer reasonable? Did you answer the question asked?

Worked Example 1: Heart rate from a short count

A nurse counts 18 heartbeats in 15 seconds. What is the heart rate in beats per minute?

1 minute = 60 s, and 60 ÷ 15 = 4. So 18 × 4 = 72 beats per minute.

Worked Example 2: Dose by body mass

A medicine (약) is given at 10 mg for every kg of body mass. How much for a 32 kg child?

10 mg/kg × 32 kg = 320 mg. (The "kg" units cancel, leaving mg.)

Worked Example 3: Using a ratio

A sports drink is mixed 1 scoop of powder : 4 cups of water. How many scoops for 20 cups?

20 ÷ 4 = 5 groups of 4 cups, so 5 scoops.

Worked Example 4: Mean hours of sleep

Last week you slept 8, 7, 9, 6, 8 hours on school nights. What is the mean?

Sum = 38. Mean = 38 ÷ 5 = 7.6 hours.

Worked Example 5: Steps to kilometres

One of your steps is about 0.7 m long. How far is 5,000 steps?

5,000 × 0.7 m = 3,500 m. 1 km = 1,000 m, so 3,500 ÷ 1,000 = 3.5 km.
