🧮 Math Day 1: Kimchi Kitchen Math Lab

Welcome to the kitchen, Jacqueline! Today you'll use fractions, ratios, percentages, unit rates, area/volume, mean/median/range, BEDMAS, and elapsed time — the same skills a restaurant cook or market vendor uses when scaling recipes for kimchi, bibimbap, and bulgogi.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver:
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations, and in what order?
  3. Solve: One step at a time; write each step down.
  4. Check: Is the answer reasonable? Did you answer the question asked?

Worked Example 1: Scaling a kimchi recipe

A recipe for 4 people uses 800 g of napa cabbage. How much cabbage for 10 people?

Step 1: Unit rate = 800 ÷ 4 = 200 g per person.

Step 2: 200 × 10 = 2000 g (or 2 kg).

Worked Example 2: Fraction of rice bowls

A restaurant cooked 96 bowls of rice. Servers have plated 5/8 of them. How many bowls are plated?

96 ÷ 8 = 12; 12 × 5 = 60 bowls.

Worked Example 3: Percent of orders

Out of 80 lunch orders, 35% were bibimbap. How many were bibimbap?

0.35 × 80 = 28 orders.

Worked Example 4: Mean, median, range

Five daily kimchi jar sales: 4, 9, 6, 5, 6. Find mean, median, and range.

Mean = 30 ÷ 5 = 6. Ordered: 4, 5, 6, 6, 9 → median 6. Range = 9 − 4 = 5.

Worked Example 5: Volume & BEDMAS

A sauce tray is 50 cm × 40 cm × 5 cm. Volume = 50 × 40 × 5 = 10 000 cm³.

BEDMAS: (60 − 20) ÷ 5 × 3² + 6 → 40 ÷ 5 = 8; 3² = 9; 8 × 9 = 72; 72 + 6 = 78.
