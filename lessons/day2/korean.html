Welcome back, Jacqueline! This week your English lessons explore Chuseok, Korea's autumn harvest festival, and how Korean families talk to each other. You'll learn 10 new family words with Hangul, Revised Romanization, English meaning, and listen-and-say practice for every word. Some example sentences reuse Day 1 words (밥, 주세요, 안녕하세요, 감사합니다, 맛있어요) so you keep them fresh.

How to read Revised Romanization:
  • Letters approximate the sound in English. Korean does not use tone numbers like Cantonese.
  • Example: harabeoji — roughly ha-ra-buh-jee. The letters eo sound like the "u" in up.
  • Listening tip: Tap Hear model, say the word out loud three times, then Record and compare.

Culture tip: Korean family words depend on who is speaking. A girl calls her older sister 언니 (eonni), but a boy says 누나 (nuna). 동생 (dongsaeng) works for any younger sibling. 엄마 / 아빠 are warm, everyday words (like "Mom" and "Dad").
