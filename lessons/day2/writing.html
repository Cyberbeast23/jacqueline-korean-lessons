✍️ Writing Day 2: Problem-Solution (The Chuseok Puzzle)

Jacqueline, this week you'll write a problem-solution paragraph: name a real problem, explain why it matters, then propose solutions backed by reasons and evidence. Our topic: how families can still celebrate together when they live far apart.

Part 1: The Lesson

Problem → Why it matters → Solutions → Conclusion

A strong problem-solution paragraph does more than complain. It shows the reader the problem clearly, then offers realistic fixes and explains why they would work.

1. Problem: state it in one clear topic sentence
2. Cause / effect: why it happens and who it hurts
3. Solution 1: a practical fix + reason
4. Solution 2: another fix + example or evidence
5. Conclusion: why the solutions are worth trying

Useful linking words: because, as a result, one solution is, another option, for example, this would, therefore, however.

Model Problem-Solution Paragraph

Problem (topic sentence)Cause / effectSolutions + reasonsConclusion

For many Korean families, the hardest part of Chuseok is simply getting everyone in the same room. Relatives often live in different cities or even different countries, and as a result, holiday traffic, expensive flights, and work schedules can keep grandparents from seeing their grandchildren. One solution is to hold a "songpyeon video call," where each household folds rice cakes in its own kitchen while chatting on screen, because cooking together still feels personal even across distance. Another option is to rotate the host: each year, a different branch of the family travels, so no one has to make the long trip every time. With a little planning and some creativity, distance does not have to break the circle around the family table.

Notice: Each solution comes with a reason (because…, so…). That is what makes it convincing.
