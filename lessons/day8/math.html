🧮 Math Day 8: Time & Schedule Math

안녕하세요, Jacqueline! This week's theme is a day in the life in Seoul: waking up, catching the subway, school, 학원, dinner, and bed. Every one of those moments runs on a clock. You'll use elapsed time, 24-hour time, schedules and timetables, rates, fractions of a day, and the mean to plan a day like a Seoul commuter.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver:
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline times and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations, and in what order? Should you work in minutes?
  3. Solve: One step at a time; write each step down.
  4. Check: Is the answer reasonable? Did you answer the question asked (minutes or hours)?

Worked Example 1: Elapsed time by "jumping" on a number line

Min-jun leaves home at 7:48 a.m. and arrives at school at 8:25 a.m. How long is his trip?

Jump to the next full hour: 7:48 → 8:00 is 12 min. Then 8:00 → 8:25 is 25 min. Total = 12 + 25 = 37 minutes.

Worked Example 2: 24-hour time

Korean timetables often use the 24-hour clock. What is 18:40 in 12-hour time?

For times after 12:59, subtract 12 from the hour: 18 − 12 = 6. So 18:40 = 6:40 p.m. (저녁!).

Worked Example 3: Trains that come "every ___ minutes"

A subway train leaves every 6 minutes, starting at 7:00. How many trains leave from 7:00 to 7:30, counting both?

7:00, 7:06, 7:12, 7:18, 7:24, 7:30. That's 30 ÷ 6 = 5 gaps, so 5 + 1 = 6 trains (always add one when you count both ends!).

Worked Example 4: Working backwards

School starts at 8:40. Getting ready takes 35 min and the bus ride takes 25 min. When is the latest Jacqueline can get up (일어나요)?

Total time needed = 35 + 25 = 60 min = 1 hour. 8:40 − 1 hour = 7:40 a.m.

Worked Example 5: Fraction of a day

If you sleep (자요) 9 hours a night, what fraction of a 24-hour day is that?

9/24 = 3/8 (divide top and bottom by 3), which is 0.375 or 37.5% of the day.
