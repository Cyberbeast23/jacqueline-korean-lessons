🇰🇷 Korean Day 5: Shopping at the Market

Welcome back, Jacqueline! Grab your wallet — this week we're shopping at a Korean traditional market (시장). You'll learn 10 new shopping words with Hangul, Revised Romanization, English meaning, and listen-and-say practice for every word. By the end you can ask 이거 얼마예요? (How much is this?), say whether something is cheap or expensive, buy fruit, and ask to pay by card. Example sentences reuse earlier words (주세요, 맛있어요, 엄마, 감사합니다) so you keep them fresh.

How to read Revised Romanization:
  • Letters approximate the sound in English. Korean does not use tone numbers like Cantonese.
  • Example: eolmayeyo — roughly uhl-ma-yeh-yo. eo is an open "uh" like in up, and the final -yo makes it polite.
  • Tense consonants: ㅆ (ss) in 싸요 and 비싸요 is a "tight" s — tense your throat and say it sharply, like the hiss in sssnake. Compare soft ㅅ (s) in 사요 (I buy) and 사과 (apple). 싸요 = cheap, 사요 = I buy — one tiny sound changes the meaning!
  • Sound change: 과일 is written gwa + il and said gwail, but when 이 follows, the ㄹ slides over and turns soft: 과일이 = gwairi (the ㄹ sounds like a quick tap, almost a light "r").
  • Listening tip: Tap Hear model, say the word out loud three times, then Record and compare.

Culture tip: Korea's traditional markets, like Namdaemun and Gwangjang Market in Seoul, are packed with tiny stalls selling fruit, fabric, kitchenware, and street food such as 빈대떡 (mung-bean pancakes) and 떡볶이 (spicy rice cakes). Prices are in won (₩), and most stalls take cards, though small vendors love cash. Friendly sellers sometimes add a little extra for free — this is called 덤 (deom), a "bonus" for a loyal customer. Hand over money and receive change with both hands (or your right hand with your left touching your wrist) to show respect.
