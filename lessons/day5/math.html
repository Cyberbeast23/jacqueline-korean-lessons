🧮 Math Day 5: Market Day Math

안녕하세요, Jacqueline! This week you're shopping at a busy Korean traditional market (시장). You'll use unit prices, best-buy comparisons, percent discounts and increases, making change, ratios, converting won to Canadian dollars, equations, and the mean to shop smart.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver:
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline numbers and units.
  2. Plan: Which operations, and in what order?
  3. Solve: One step at a time; write each step down.
  4. Check: Is the answer reasonable? Did you answer the question asked?

Worked Example 1: Unit price (the best buy)

Stall A sells 4 apples for ₩6,000. Stall B sells 6 apples for ₩8,400. Which is the better deal?

Unit price = total ÷ number of items. A: 6,000 ÷ 4 = ₩1,500 per apple. B: 8,400 ÷ 6 = ₩1,400 per apple.

The lower unit price wins: Stall B is cheaper per apple.

Worked Example 2: Percent discount

A scarf costs ₩30,000 and is 20% off. What is the sale price?

Discount = 20% × 30,000 = 0.20 × 30,000 = ₩6,000.

Sale price = 30,000 − 6,000 = ₩24,000. (Shortcut: pay 80% → 0.80 × 30,000.)

Worked Example 3: Making change

You buy items costing ₩3,500 and ₩4,200 and pay with a ₩10,000 bill. How much change do you get?

Total = 3,500 + 4,200 = ₩7,700.

Change = 10,000 − 7,700 = ₩2,300. Tip: count up from 7,700 → 8,000 (300) → 10,000 (2,000).

Worked Example 4: Converting currency

We use an example rate of 1 CAD = 1,000 KRW (real rates change every day). A bag of tangerines is ₩7,500.

Won → dollars: divide by 1,000. 7,500 ÷ 1,000 = $7.50 CAD.

Worked Example 5: Solving a price equation

3 rice cakes and a ₩2,000 drink cost ₩8,000 together. What does one rice cake cost? 3x + 2000 = 8000

Subtract 2,000: 3x = 6,000. Divide by 3: x = ₩2,000.
