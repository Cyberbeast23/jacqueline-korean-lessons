✍️ Writing Day 5: Descriptive Writing (A Walk Through the Market)

Jacqueline, this week's descriptive challenge is to take your reader on a walk through a bustling Korean market. You already know senses and similes. The new skill is spatial order: guiding the reader through a place step by step (from the entrance, then down the row, then at the far end) so the description feels like a journey instead of a jumble.

Part 1: The Lesson

Describe = walk the reader through the place

Spatial order means describing things in the order you would meet them in space: near to far, left to right, entrance to exit. Use place transitions to signal each move, and keep using sensory details, a simile, and strong verbs (sizzle, weave, tumble).

See: pyramids of red apples, glowing orange persimmons, hanging lanterns
Hear: sellers calling "싸요, 싸요!", oil sizzling, cart wheels rattling
Smell: sesame oil, roasted chestnuts, spicy gochujang
Touch: a cold, smooth pear, crinkly plastic bags, warm coins
Taste: a crispy mung-bean pancake, sweet rice punch (sikhye)

Place transitions: just inside the entrance, to my left, across the aisle, further down, beyond, at the far end, above, below.

Model Descriptive Paragraph

Topic sentenceSensory details in spatial orderSimile + strong verbsConclusion

Walking into Gwangjang Market on a Saturday morning feels like stepping into a giant, noisy kitchen. Just inside the entrance, a fruit seller has stacked red apples into neat pyramids, and he calls out, "싸요, 싸요!" to everyone who passes. Further down the aisle, mung-bean pancakes sizzle in shallow pans of oil, and the smell of sesame drifts over the crowd like a warm blanket. To my left, a grandmother squeezes a cold, smooth pear to test it, while above us strings of lanterns sway in the breeze from the doorway. By the time I reach the far end, my arms are heavy with crinkly bags, and my ears are still ringing with the happy music of bargaining.

Notice: The transitions (just inside the entrance, further down, to my left, at the far end) move the reader through the market one step at a time.

For each sentence, choose whether it is mainly descriptive (vivid senses/details), plain/telling (vague), or off-topic.
