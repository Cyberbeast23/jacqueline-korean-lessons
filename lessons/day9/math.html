🧮 Math Day 9: Zookeeper Math

안녕하세요, Jacqueline! This week's theme is 동물 (animals). Today you're an assistant zookeeper at a 동물원 (zoo) in Seoul: feeding the 호랑이, measuring the 토끼 pen, filling the 물고기 tank, and tracking a famous Jindo dog's journey. You'll use rates, unit conversion, perimeter and area, volume, ratios, percent, and the mean, just like real zookeepers do every day.

Part 1: Learn the Moves

The 4-Step Problem Solver:
  1. Read: What is the question actually asking? Underline the numbers and the units.
  2. Plan: Which operations, and in what order? Do the units match (kg or g, cm or m)?
  3. Solve: One step at a time; write each step down.
  4. Check: Is the answer reasonable? A rabbit doesn't eat 40 kg a day!

Worked Example 1: Rates (per day → per week)

A 곰 (bear) eats 12 kg of food a day. How much does it eat in one week?

Rate × time: 12 kg/day × 7 days = 84 kg per week.

Worked Example 2: Perimeter vs. area

A 토끼 (rabbit) pen is a rectangle 6 m long and 4 m wide. How much fence goes around it, and how much ground is inside?

Perimeter (fence) = 2 × (6 + 4) = 20 m. Area (ground) = 6 × 4 = 24 m². Fence = around the edge; area = the space inside.

Worked Example 3: Volume and litres

A 물고기 (fish) tank is 50 cm × 30 cm × 40 cm. How many litres of water does it hold when full?

Volume = 50 × 30 × 40 = 60 000 cm³. Since 1 L = 1000 cm³, that's 60 000 ÷ 1000 = 60 L.

Worked Example 4: Ratios

The zoo's birdhouse has 새 (birds) in a ratio of parrots : finches = 2 : 5. There are 35 birds in all. How many are parrots?

2 + 5 = 7 parts. 35 ÷ 7 = 5 birds per part. Parrots = 2 × 5 = 10.

Worked Example 5: Percent of a number

A 호랑이 (tiger) weighs 200 kg. It eats about 4% of its body weight in meat on a feeding day. How much is that?

4% = 0.04. 0.04 × 200 = 8 kg of meat.
