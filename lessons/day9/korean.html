🇰🇷 Korean Day 9: Animals & the Zoo

Welcome back, Jacqueline! This week is all about 동물 (dongmul, animals): pets at home, animals at the 동물원 (zoo), and the famous animals of Korean stories. You'll learn 10 new words with Hangul, Revised Romanization, English meaning, and listen-and-say practice for every word. By the end you can name eight animals, say you like animals, tell someone you're going to the zoo, and say 귀여워요! (so cute!). Example sentences reuse earlier words (자요, 아침, 겨울, 가족, 이름) so you keep them fresh.

How to read Revised Romanization:
  • Letters approximate the sound in English. Korean does not use tone numbers like Cantonese.
  • Example: gwiyeowoyo — roughly gwee-yuh-wuh-yo. wi glides like the "wee" in week, and eo is the open "uh" sound you already know from 저녁.
  • ㄲ in tokki (토끼) is a tense k: say it tight and sharp, with no puff of air, like the "k" in sky. Compare ㅋ (k with a big puff of air) in 카드.
  • Sound change: in 동물원, the final ㄹ slides into 원, so it is said dong-mu-rwon and Revised Romanization writes it dongmurwon. The same thing happens in 동물을 = dongmureul.
  • Animal sounds are different in Korean! Dogs say 멍멍 (meongmeong), cats say 야옹 (yaong), and little birds say 짹짹 (jjaekjjaek). To ask what an animal is, say 이게 뭐예요? (ige mwoyeyo? what is this?).

Culture tip: The 호랑이 (tiger) is everywhere in Korean culture. Old folktales often begin "Long, long ago, when tigers smoked pipes…" (호랑이 담배 피우던 시절), the Korean way of saying "once upon a time," and the 2018 PyeongChang Winter Olympics mascot, Soohorang, was a white tiger. Korea is also proud of the 진돗개 (Jindo dog) from Jindo Island, a national treasure famous for its loyalty (you'll meet one in this week's reading!). And the 곰 (bear) appears in Korea's founding legend of Dangun, in which a bear patiently becomes a woman.
