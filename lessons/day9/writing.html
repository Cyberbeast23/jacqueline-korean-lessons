✍️ Writing Day 9: Descriptive Writing (Bring an Animal to Life)

Jacqueline, it's descriptive week again! This time your subject is alive: an animal, like Baekgu the Jindo dog from the reading, a 호랑이 pacing at the 동물원, or a pet you know. You already know senses, similes, spatial order, and mood. The new skills are precise nouns, strong verbs, and personification, plus "zooming in" on one small, surprising detail so your reader can see the animal move.

Part 1: The Lesson

Describe = make the animal move on the page

Precise nouns name exactly what you mean ("a white Jindo dog," not "an animal"). Strong verbs do the work of three weak words ("the tiger prowled," not "the tiger walked around slowly"). Personification gives a human quality to an animal or thing ("the old bear shrugged off the snow"). And zooming in means choosing one tiny detail (a twitching whisker, a muddy paw print) instead of a long list of everything.

See: amber eyes, orange fur striped like brushstrokes, a tail flicking like a whip
Hear: a low rumbling growl, claws clicking on rock, a sudden splash
Smell: damp straw, wet fur, fresh-cut hay
Touch: a cold, wet nose, rough tongue, fur as soft as a winter scarf
Movement: prowl, pounce, slink, scamper, waddle, dart, stretch, nuzzle

Weak → strong: "The rabbit moved quickly" → "The rabbit darted." "The dog was very tired" → "The dog sagged against the gate, ribs heaving." Avoid empty words: very, really, nice, cute, big, went, got.

Model Descriptive Paragraph

Topic sentencePrecise nouns + zoomed-in detailStrong verbs + personificationConclusion

Behind the thick glass at the Seoul Grand Park Zoo, the Siberian tiger ruled her rocky hill like a queen inspecting her kingdom. Her orange coat was slashed with black stripes, and her huge paws, each as wide as a dinner plate, left damp prints on the stone. She prowled to the edge of the pool, lowered her head, and lapped the water with a long pink tongue. Then she lifted her eyes and stared straight at me, as if she were bored by one more noisy visitor. One white whisker twitched. Her tail flicked once, twice. When she finally yawned, showing teeth as long as my fingers, the whole crowd stepped back, and I understood why Koreans have told stories about the 호랑이 for a thousand years.

Notice: The writer never says "the tiger was big and scary." Precise nouns (paws, whisker, pool), strong verbs (prowled, lapped, flicked), personification (bored, queen inspecting her kingdom), and one zoomed-in detail (a single twitching whisker) let the reader see it.

Part 2: Vivid or Vague?

For each sentence, choose whether it is vivid and precise (strong verbs, exact details), vague (plain, tells instead of shows), or off-topic.
